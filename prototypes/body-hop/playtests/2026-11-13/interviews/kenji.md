# Kenji — exit interview (Body Hop)

1. **What it's about:** You play a colourless spirit that hops into sleeping bodies. Each body sees the world differently, so the level changes depending on whose eyes you're using. Bodies you leave behind stay where they are, so they become your weights, doorstops and relays.

2. **Rules as I understood them:**
- Walk into a body to hop into it. That costs one turn and the body you leave stays put, asleep.
- You can only hop to a body orthogonally adjacent to the one you're in. You can't step out as a bare spirit.
- The spirit can't pass any coloured tile and weighs nothing.
- The guard sees red: red blocks are walls, blue blocks don't exist for it. It carries the key for doors.
- The cat sees blue: blue blocks are walls, red ones don't exist. Only the cat fits through vents.
- Dashed outlines are things that exist for other bodies but not for you.
- A body on a plate opens every gate. A body standing in any gate holds every gate open.
- Parked bodies block movement, and walking into one is a hop, not a push.
- A gate closing doesn't matter if nobody needs it at that moment.
- Par is the optimal turn count.

3. **Best:** Level 11, "Two breaks". Three bodies circle a ring, and the cat you send to evict the guard ends up being the one that escapes. Level 12 was a close second because the gate is allowed to close for a moment.

4. **Worst:** Levels 1–5 hand you the solution. Level 9's hint ("what happens when you hop in?") promised a twist I never felt. Level 10 was mostly shuffling.

5. **Where I stopped:** After level 12. N wrapped back to level 1 with no ending screen.

6. **Confusion:** The striped tile on level 5. The legend (L) cleared it up right away. I still don't know what the hint on level 9 meant.

7. **Difficulty (1–5):** 1:1, 2:1, 3:1, 4:1, 5:1, 6:2, 7:2, 8:3, 9:2, 10:3, 11:3.5, 12:3.5. Real aha moments came on 8 (hand the plate to a gate-holder), 11 and 12. I got every level at par on my first plan and never used undo.

8. **Ratings:** Gameplay 4, Graphics 3 (clean, and the perspective re-tint is great), Audio 1 (I heard nothing), Innovation 4, Theme "SHIFT" 4 (shifting bodies and shifting perception).

9. **Compared to the genre's best:** The core idea is honestly good. Seeing a single map through different hosts' eyes has the same seed as Parabox or Baba. But it stops right where Snakebird would start getting cruel. Every level gave in to straightforward forward planning, and the hints often gave away the key idea. **The one fix:** add 5–8 harder levels where the obvious plan fails, with more bodies and maybe a third perception, and cut the hint text that names the trick.

AI persona: jonas (game jam judge, ~15 minutes per game)

## What the game is about
A small puzzle game where you are a white spirit that hops into other bodies (guard, cat). Each body sees different colours and can pass different tiles. You get to the EXIT by chaining bodies.

## Rules as I understood them
- Walk into a sleeping body to hop into it. Confirmed.
- The guard carries the key and can pass locked doors. Confirmed.
- The cat ignores red blocks (shown dashed) and can cross the blue bridge. The guard and the spirit see the bridge as a pit. Confirmed.
- Hopping leaves the old body standing where it was. Confirmed, and it bit me.
- Walking into a parked body hops you back into it, even if you just wanted to walk past. Confirmed by accident.
- Par is a turn count. Guessed: it is only a score target.
- Legend (L) lists what each body can and cannot pass. Confirmed. It is useful.

## Best moment
Level 2, when the red walls turned out to be nothing to the cat and the cat walked straight through. Level 3 then used the same idea with the bridge, and that was a clean "oh, I get it".

## Worst moment
Level 2, where I wasted about 10 calls and finished at 28 turns against a par of 12. Two things went wrong. First, I left the guard parked in the cat's path, so I kept hopping back into the guard. Second, a long key string after a hop seemed to lose inputs. Turn counts also went up when I wasn't sure what had moved.

## Where I got stuck
Level 2. I couldn't tell which body I was controlling after multi-key sequences. The "Back in the cat" and "You are the cat now" messages helped, but only after the fact.

## Difficulty and satisfaction
- Level 1: difficulty 1, satisfaction 4. Clear, tidy tutorial, par on the first try.
- Level 2: difficulty 3, satisfaction 3. The idea is good. My own mistakes with body placement spoiled it, and the screen goes tinted red while you are the guard, which is a bit confusing.
- Level 3: difficulty 2, satisfaction 4. Par on the first try, and it made me feel clever.

## Would I play on
Yes, for a few more levels. The hopping idea is fresh and levels are short. I stopped because my judging time was up, not because I was frustrated. I'd want a clearer cue for which body is active and for where hopping leaves the old body.

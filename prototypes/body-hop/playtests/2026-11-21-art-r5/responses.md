## ArtCritic4

## 1. Cohesion and polish: 7/10

**What works:** the palette is tight and consistent, and the brick walls are recoloured per world (grey, brown, blue), so I always know which world I'm in. The characters read at a glance. The bright exit door and green EXIT sign are clear goals. The active character gets a yellow outline and sleeping ones get a "Z", which is easy to learn. In the colour-blind shots (d1–d3) the hatched blocks and dotted blocks still tell apart.

**What holds it back:** the floor has very low contrast, there are too many small overlays crowding single tiles, and a few elements are visually ambiguous.

## 2. Per element

- **Characters:** they have charm and read well. The pink-cheek cat variant at (2,3) in c10 is cute. The Z's and outlines spill into neighbouring tiles and overlap other characters (c3 at (6,1)–(6,2), c7 at (7,2)–(7,3)).
- **Walls:** the bricks and the dark top lip look good. The inner wall stubs, such as (5,2)–(5,3) in c1, look flat.
- **Floor:** the large slabs with cracks are fine. They're nearly the same value as faded blocks and the walls in the grey world (c1, c10).
- **Coloured blocks:** red hatched blocks and blue dotted blocks, with three states (solid, faded, dashed outline), make a strong system. The faded state in c1 is too close to the floor. In the colour-blind shots, red turns olive, the same hue as the player and the active outline.
- **Grey blocks:** the steel box with a circle is crisp. It reads as a safe or a button, not as something you push.
- **Plates:** I don't know which sprite is a plate. My best guess is the green corner marks under the player in c5 and c6.
- **Gates:** the bars with red lights (closed) versus green lights (open, bars raised) are clear and nice.
- **Exit:** clear. When a gate sits right above the exit (c4, c9), the EXIT sign is drawn over the gate's lower half.
- **Markers:** the Z and the yellow outline work. I don't know what the yellow triangle means (c6, c8, c10).

## 3. Top 5 improvements, ranked

1. Make pressure plates a clear, distinct sprite that stays visible when unpressed.
2. Raise floor-to-block contrast, especially for faded blocks in the grey world.
3. Keep Z's and arrows inside their own tile, or layer them above neighbours cleanly.
4. Give the grey pushable block a crate-like, pushable look and drop the target-like circle.
5. Give the exit sign its own wall tile so it never overlaps a gate. Also make the colour-blind red more distinct from the gold outline.

# Exit interview: Kenji (puzzle expert)

## What the game is about
You're a weightless spirit that hops into sleeping bodies (guards and cats) to get one of them to the exit. Each body sees colours differently, so the same tile is a wall for one body and empty floor for another. When you hop out of a body it stays where it was. That leftover body is the real puzzle piece. Most of the later levels come down to routing control: who stands next to whom when you need to hop.

## Rules as I understood them
- Walking into an adjacent body moves you into it. The body you leave stays put. (confirmed)
- Hops work from any tile, including doorways, red blocks a cat is standing in, and bridges. (confirmed, used on 4, X3, X4, X6)
- Guards carry a key and open brown locked doors by walking into them. (confirmed)
- Guards see red, so red blocks are walls to them and red bridges are floor. Blue blocks are empty to them and blue bridges are pits. (confirmed)
- Cats are the reverse: red blocks and red bridges aren't there for them, blue blocks are walls, blue bridges are floor. (confirmed)
- The spirit sees no colour. Every coloured block is fog (a wall) and every bridge is a pit. It weighs nothing, so it doesn't press plates. (confirmed on level 10)
- Plates hold all gates open while a body stands on one. (confirmed)
- A body standing in a gate holds all gates open, even with the plate empty. (confirmed)
- A body can step straight from a plate into an adjacent gate and the gate stays open because the body is now in it. (confirmed on X5, and the game showed a message for it)
- Vents let only cats through. (guessed. I only ever sent cats through them, so I never tested a guard.)
- The spirit vanishes once you hop. There's no free spirit afterwards. (guessed from the screenshots)

## Best moment
X5 "Shift change". I couldn't see how anyone could be standing next to the plate when someone stepped onto it, since the only way onto it went through the tile where the helper would need to wait. Then I tried stepping from the plate straight into the gate so the cat props it open itself. After that the swap fell out. Close second: X6, where the guard has to cross east and then the other guard walks all the way back west over the red bridge to sit on the plate.

## Worst moment
Level 12, my first attempt. I moved the guard sideways and then sent the cat "up" onto where the guard used to be. Entirely my own counting error, but the board doesn't remind you which bodies have moved, and it cost me a restart. Small gripe: X1 is mostly walking. The thinking is one step and then you do 13 moves of corridor.

## Where I got stuck
- Level 12 for one restart, from the counting slip above.
- X5 needed the longest planning. I went through several control-flow dead ends before trying plate-to-gate.
- X6 took a lot of planning too, but my first full plan worked.

## Would I play on?
Yes. The main game is too gentle for me until level 12, but Night shift is good: tight and fair, and the hints point at the right question without giving the answer away. I'd play more levels at X5/X6 difficulty. Par was always exactly my optimal count, so the par numbers feel trustworthy. They also work as an unintended check: when my count came in under par on 12, I knew I'd misread something.

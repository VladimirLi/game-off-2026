# Kenji: exit interview, Body Hop

**1. What it's about.** You're a weightless spirit that possesses bodies. Each body sees the world through a different colour filter, and every body you leave stays behind asleep where you left it. In the end it's a puzzle about passing control from body to body, using the bodies you abandon as paperweights, doorstops and stepping stones.

**2. Rules as I understood them.**
- You hop into a body by walking into it. You can hop between any two adjacent bodies, wherever each one stands: in a doorway, in a gate, or inside a wall.
- The spirit vanishes when it hops, so it's the only thing that leaves no trace. It's weightless, it can't press plates, and it can't pass anything coloured.
- Guard: red blocks are walls to it and red bridges are floor. It carries the key, so it can open doors, and it can walk through blue walls.
- Cat: blue blocks are walls and blue bridges are floor. It walks through red walls, and it's the only body that fits through vents.
- A dashed outline means the tile is there for other bodies, not for you.
- A plate holds the gates open while a body stands on it. A body standing in any gate holds every gate open.
- Parked bodies block movement, so walking into one hops you into it.

**3. Best moment / level.** X6 "Go-between". Working out that the spirit has to be spent on the cat, because anyone else wedges it into its alcove, and that the guard must park inside the blue wall so the cat can reach it across the blue bridge. X4's "exit hatch", pre-parking a cat in the red cell beside the guard's pocket, was a close second.

**4. Worst moment / level.** Level 3. I batched my moves and the cat stepped straight back into the guard I had just left. That was fair, but the game hadn't yet shown me that left-behind bodies persist. As a level, X1 was the worst: a smart setup, then a 13-step lap of pure walking.

**5. Where I stopped.** Nowhere. I finished all 12 main levels plus X1 to X6, every one at par.

**6. Confusion.** I didn't know what the horizontal-bar tile was until I opened the legend at level 7. After that it was clear. The legend should pop up the first time a new tile appears.

**7. Difficulty.** 1-3: 1. 4: 2. 5-7: 1. 8: 2. 9-10: 1. 11: 2. 12: 3. X1: 2. X2: 3. X3: 3. X4: 4. X5: 4. X6: 4.
Real ahas: 12, X2, X4, X5, X6. The hints for 5 to 10 basically give the answer away.

**8. Ratings.** Gameplay 4, Graphics 3, Audio 1 (I heard nothing), Innovation 4, Theme 4. You shift bodies and perspectives, and there's literally a "shift change".

**9. Compared to the genre's best.** It's clean and rule-consistent, closer to Snakebird-lite than to Stephen's Sausage Roll. The main game is a tutorial. The real game is in Night shift, and the walking laps dilute it. The one thing I'd change is to cut the busywork: tighten the maps so every move is a decision, and make the X-levels the main campaign.

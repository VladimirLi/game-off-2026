# Exit interview: Sam (methodical engineer)

## What the game is about
You play a weightless spirit that hops into sleeping bodies (a guard and cats). Each body sees colours differently, so the same tile is a wall for one body and open floor for another. Bodies you leave stay where they are, and the puzzles are about putting those leftover bodies in the right places: on plates, in doorways, or next to each other so you can hop between them.

## Rules as I understood them
- You move one tile per turn. Walking into an adjacent body hops you into it, and the hop costs one turn. (confirmed)
- The spirit can't go through red, blue or pits, and weighs nothing, so it doesn't press plates. (confirmed for plates and red; for blue I'm guessing)
- The guard sees red, so red blocks are walls to it. It doesn't see blue, so blue striped blocks are open floor and a blue bridge is a pit. (confirmed)
- The cat can't see red, so it walks through red blocks. It sees blue, so it can use the blue bridge and treats blue blocks as walls. (confirmed)
- Guards carry the key and open padlocked doors by walking into them. (confirmed)
- You can hop between any two adjacent bodies wherever they stand, including a doorway or inside a coloured block. (confirmed)
- Bodies you leave stay put and are marked with footprints. (confirmed)
- A plate opens every gate while a body stands on it. A body standing in a gate also holds them all open. All gates open and close together. (confirmed)
- Only a cat fits through a vent. (taken from the hint; never tested with a guard)

## Best moment
Level 8, Bucket brigade. Passing control down a chain (cat on red, then the guard, then the lower cat onto the plate) and back up again without anyone taking a step felt like real engineering. Level 12 used the same relay idea at a bigger scale, and working it out on paper first and then hitting par exactly was very satisfying.

## Worst moment
Nothing really went wrong. The closest was level 6. The hint asks "where will the cat sleep?", which made me think the cat had to end up on the red block. My plan left it on plain floor beside the red, and that also worked, so the hint pointed at a tighter constraint than the level actually had. A small mismatch, not a problem.

## Where I got stuck
Nowhere for long. Level 12 took the most planning: I had to see that the guard leaving the plate needs one cat waiting on the red next to the plate and a second cat as a relay above it. Level 10's hint ("who could wake it and leave the plate empty") gave the key idea, that the spirit can cross the plate, a bit too directly.

## Would I play on?
Yes. The rules are consistent and they stack in a way I can reason about on paper, which is what I want. I'd keep going through Night shift. If anything, I'd like the main game to get harder sooner. Levels 1 to 7 were mostly single-idea tutorials.

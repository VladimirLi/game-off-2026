# Exit interview: Kenji (puzzle expert)

## What the game is about
You're a weightless spirit that possesses sleeping bodies (guards, cats) by walking into them. Each body sees colours differently, so the same tile is a wall for one body and empty space for another. Bodies you leave behind stay where they are, and you use them as paperweights on plates, gate holders, or relay points for your next hop. The whole game is about deciding who stands where, and in what order.

## Rules as I understood them
- Walking into an adjacent body hops you into it. The body you left stays put. (confirmed)
- Hopping works wherever either body stands, including inside a wall tile or in a doorway. (confirmed, L4 and L11)
- Guards carry the key; locked doors open for a guard. (confirmed)
- Guard sees red (red blocks are walls) and not blue (blue is open floor for it). Cat sees blue, not red: the reverse. (confirmed)
- The spirit is blocked by red, and blue bridges are pits to it. (confirmed for red; guessed for blue walls)
- Plates hold all gates open while a body stands on them. The spirit weighs nothing. (confirmed, L10 relies on it)
- A body standing in a gate holds every gate open. (confirmed L9)
- Only cats fit through vents. (confirmed by the text; I never tried to put a guard in one)
- Once you've hopped, you can't go back to being a spirit. (guessed, never tested)
- Hopping costs one turn, the same as a step. (confirmed by par counts)

## Best moment
X2 "Knot". I had to work out the order to untangle three cats into the bottom row behind the guard, with each cat ending next to the body I needed to hop into next. It came out at exactly par on the first try. L11 "Lay-by" was also great: parking a cat inside a red block and hopping from there back into a guard standing in a blue block.

## Worst moment
L12. I counted the spirit as one tile from the first cat when it was two, so my whole sequence was shifted by one and I ended up in a cat instead of the guard. That was my mistake, not the game's, but it was the one place the grid was hard to read at a glance. I restarted and got par. Apart from that, the hint text on most levels gives away the key idea ("Who could wake it and leave the plate empty?"), so the main game felt easier than it needed to be for me.

## Where I got stuck
Never really stuck. The miscount on L12 cost one restart.

## Would I play on?
Yes. Night shift is where the game gets interesting for someone like me, and I'd go on to X3-X6. I'd like an option to hide the hint lines, or to show them only after a few failed attempts. The mechanics are tight and readable, and the colour-vision idea has depth the main levels only start to use.

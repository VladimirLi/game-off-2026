# Slide the Row (grey box)

Your yellow piece sits fixed in the centre of a 7x7 grid. Move a cursor to any row or column and slide it one cell with wrap-around. Win when the green goal slides under you. A wall can never slide under you, so that shift is blocked. A red hazard can slide under you, and then you lose (Z undo, R restart).

Run: `godot --path .`

Controls: Arrows/WASD move the cursor. Shift+Arrows slide the cursor's row (left/right) or column (up/down). Z undo, R restart, N next level, Enter start.

Levels: 8, generated by scrambling a solved state with K legal shifts (K = 3 to 14, walls 4 to 18, hazards 0 to 2). Seeds are fixed. Par is K, an upper bound; the true optimum can be lower.

Design note: the brief said "shift the player's own row or column". With a fixed piece that only ever moves the centre row and column, so the goal is at most two shifts away and walls do nothing. The cursor makes any line shiftable, which is the original idea's verb.

## The question

Does shifting the world around a fixed piece feel clever and readable, or confusing?

## What fun would look like

- The player pauses, traces a wall's path around the wrap, then sees "move that row first to clear the column" and it works.
- Blocked shifts read as a clear rule, not as a bug.
- Players beat par sometimes and want to replay to match it.
- Failure would look like random cursor flailing until the goal happens to arrive, or losing track of where things went after a wrap.

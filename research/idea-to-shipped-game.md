# From game idea to shipped jam game: prototype, slice, playtest, finish

Researched 2026-11-07. Scope: solo developer, experienced in software, new to game development, Godot 4, browser-playable, 3-week jam (Game Off 2026, due Dec 1). Theme-to-idea, jam rules, rating mechanics and Bevy are covered in `game-off-2026-how-to-win.md` and not repeated. Claims cite primary sources (talks, books, the developers' own posts, engine docs); anything marked *unverified* could not be traced to a primary record. Several talks are only on video; where a claim rests on a secondary write-up of a talk, that is said.

## The 12 rules

1. **Prototype the one mechanic as a question, not a demo.** A good prototype is "testable, falsifiable, quick to make, relevant, surprising" and "prototypes ask questions"; prototype code need not be "robust, elegant, or optimal" (Hecker and Gingold, GDC 2006, via [Juul's notes](https://www.jesperjuul.net/ludologist/?p=251)). Schell: "the more times you test and improve your design, the better your game will be" ([Rule of the Loop](https://notesbylex.com/rule-of-loop)).
2. **Follow the mechanic where it leads; cut what you have to force.** Blow: games are "biased toward revealing truth, so long as we do not quash the truth in order to force our own high-level wishes into the design" ([GDC Europe 2011](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-jon-blow-on-the-truth-in-game-design-)). Berry on Celeste: many chapters had "mechanics, and levels that were cut" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2018/01/feature_conquering_the_indie_mountain_with_celeste_creator_matt_makes_games)).
3. **Judge scope by what you have already finished, not by your current skill.** Derek Yu's advice for escaping the "Loop of Restarting" ([Death Loops](https://www.derekyu.com/makegames/deathloops.html)); start "very small in scope", with little one-off content and a familiar genre ([Assessing Risk](https://www.derekyu.com/makegames/risk.html)).
4. **Make it playable start to finish before polishing anything.** Yu: make the whole game playable "before refining any single part" ([Death Loops](https://www.derekyu.com/makegames/deathloops.html)). 25-jam veteran: "get the gameplay loop working as soon as possible" ([Topielski](https://jontopielski.itch.io/hearts-ablaze/devlog/377861/5-takeaways-from-doing-25-game-jams)).
5. **Export the web build to itch.io in week 1 and keep exporting.** The single most repeated postmortem failure. "Test your target platform early and often" after "virtually none of my core mechanics worked in the final build" (Godot, GMTK 2025, [Err Friday](https://itch.io/devlog/1003353/gmtk-game-jam-2025-post-mortem-when-everything-breaks-and-how-to-fix-it.amp)); Blastsmith could not test its WebGL build before upload ([postmortem](https://zameleon.itch.io/blastsmith/devlog/94156/crafting-blastsmith-4-things-that-went-right-and-4-things-that-went-wrong)).
6. **Playtest from the first week, in silence, watching bodies not words.** Valve started Portal playtests one week in; Swift: "you'll really tell by their body language"; Speyrer: iterate until it is "no longer excruciatingly painful to watch the playtests" ([Valve quotes via GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/valves-secret-weapon)). Yu: players "will know HOW they feel about your game, but they may not necessarily know WHY" ([Getting Feedback](https://www.derekyu.com/makegames/feedback.html)).
7. **Teach each mechanic with a level: introduce, develop, twist, conclude.** Hayashida: "decide on a core concept in level design", then "a slightly more complicated scenario in which you have to use it", in the four-panel kishōtenketsu shape ([Game Developer 2012](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-secret-to-i-mario-i-level-design)). Portal's commentary: the first room trains button and box before portals ([Portal commentary](https://combineoverwiki.net/wiki/Developer_commentary/Portal)).
8. **Make difficulty fair by cheating in the player's favour.** Celeste: "we want the game to feel hard, we always want it to feel fair", "the game works in the player's favor" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2018/01/feature_conquering_the_indie_mountain_with_celeste_creator_matt_makes_games)); Canabalt's hitbox is "more forgiving" and offset toward the runner's back ([Tuning Canabalt](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/tuning-canabalt)).
9. **Budget the last third for juice and audio, and get audio in before the final day.** "Juice it or lose it" (GDC Europe 2012) and "The Art of Screenshake" (2013) are both demos of ~30 small tweaks turning a dull prototype into a satisfying one ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1016487/Juice-It-or-Lose), [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games)). Godot's own tutorial: sound and music "can be the single most effective way to add appeal" ([Finishing up](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/getting_started/first_2d_game/07.finishing-up.html)). A team that swapped in large .wav files on the last day got "30-40 min load times" on web and missed the deadline ([Vase](https://itch.io/devlog/666664/submission-build-audio-issues-postmortem-from-vase.amp)).
10. **Keep a normal sleep schedule.** Two solo Ludum Dare postmortems independently conclude they would have been "more productive overall, and happier" sticking to normal sleep ([LD44](https://itch.io/devlog/79103/ludum-dare-44-postmortem-the-devil-and-some-aliens-went-down-to-georgia.amp), [LD27](https://itch.io/devlog/47486/post-ludum-dare-post-mortem-27th-overall.amp)).
11. **Use Godot 4.7.x, single-threaded web export, Compatibility renderer, GDScript.** Current stable is 4.7.2 (18 Aug 2026) ([archive](https://godotengine.org/download/archive/)). Single-threaded export is "the preferred and now default way to export your games on the Web" and needs no SharedArrayBuffer headers; C# cannot export to web ([Exporting for the Web](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)).
12. **Automate the export with godot-ci on day 1.** The reference GitHub Actions workflow runs `godot --headless --export-release "Web"` in the `barichello/godot-ci` container and deploys to GitHub Pages ([godot-ci](https://github.com/abarichello/godot-ci), [workflow](https://github.com/abarichello/godot-ci/blob/master/.github/workflows/godot-ci.yml)). Pin the image tag to your editor version.

## 1. Prototyping: is the core mechanic fun?

**Hecker and Gingold, "Advanced Prototyping", GDC 2006** ([GDC Vault listing](https://gdcvault.com/play/1013252/Advanced); Hecker's own page was down on 2026-11-07). Juul's contemporaneous notes record the traits of a good prototype: testable, falsifiable ("it must make a claim"), quick to make, relevant, surprising, persuasive; "prototypes ask questions". Pitfalls: decomposition ("deciding what part of the game to prototype, and how" is the hard part), over-engineering, and frameworks, because "frameworks want to own the project" ([notes](https://www.jesperjuul.net/ludologist/?p=251)).

**Schell, The Art of Game Design.** The Rule of the Loop: "The more times you test and improve your design, the better your game will be." Associated advice: choose a prototype form that surfaces design issues quickly, make each loop fast, prototype cheaply (paper, simpler engine) ([reader notes](https://notesbylex.com/rule-of-loop); page number varies by edition, book itself not checked).

**Blow and ten Bosch, "Designing to Reveal the Nature of the Universe", IndieCade Oct 2011.** Ten Bosch's own summary: look for "systems that express these truths in the cleanest possible way", in contrast to "combinatoric" design, by following a system's rules to their consequences ([marctenbosch.com](https://marctenbosch.com/news/?p=181)). Blow's companion GDC Europe 2011 lecture: more ideas "came out of" Braid's development than went in; observe what the mechanic produces rather than script it ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-jon-blow-on-the-truth-in-game-design-)). Video: youtube.com/watch?v=OGSeLSmOALU (*content not checked*).

**Celeste's origin.** Celeste Classic: "A PICO-8 platformer about climbing a mountain, made in four days", by Thorson and Berry ([itch page](https://maddymakesgamesinc.itch.io/celesteclassic)). Berry: "Our plan was to take our initial jam game and see where it would go if we had more time"; "The game stayed very true to the vision of the PICO-8 version" ([Nintendo Life interview](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2018/01/feature_conquering_the_indie_mountain_with_celeste_creator_matt_makes_games)). Lesson: a four-day prototype proved the mechanic before any content was built around it.

**Canabalt.** Built in about five days for the Experimental Gameplay Project ([Engadget on the GDC 2010 postmortem](https://www.engadget.com/2010/03/11/gdc-2010-canabalt-postmortem/)). Saltsman tuned by feel: "I didn't actually calculate the runner's ideal speed", he adjusted numbers through repeated play; "the faster you go, the slower you speed up" ([Tuning Canabalt](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/tuning-canabalt)).

**Derek Yu.** "Non-dev feedback is usually the best way to find out if your core ideas are working" ([2018 thread via Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-spelunky-i-designer-want-to-be-an-indie-then-learn-how-to-finish-games-reliably)). "The first games I made were prototypes that eventually led to Spelunky"; make "one sloppy, fun little game quickly and then make a game that's slightly bigger and slightly less sloppy" ([GameMaker interview, 2024](https://gamemaker.io/en/blog/derek-yu-gm25)).

**Not verified:** GMTK's "What's the Point of Prototyping?" video exists but its content could not be fetched. Sylvester's Designing Games has a section "Playtesting and Radical Revision" per a table of contents; text not checked. Blood, Sweat, and Pixels: only secondary summaries found (Stardew Valley as a solo multi-year project ending in burnout); no excerpt verified.

**Synthesis for a 3-week jam:** days 1 to 3 are the prototype. Write the question down ("is shifting X while Y fun for 2 minutes?"), build only what answers it, with placeholder art, and hand it to two people by day 3. If they do not ask to play again, change the mechanic, not the art.

## 2. Vertical slice and scope

- **Yu, "Assessing Risk":** "the common wisdom is for new developers to start very small in scope"; "The more one-off, non-reusable content you'll have to make, the more time it will take"; "the more novel your core game mechanics are, the more time it will take to get them right"; small games should still be "as big-feeling and fiddly as they can muster" ([risk](https://www.derekyu.com/makegames/risk.html)).
- **Yu, "Finishing a Game" (2010).** The post itself is on Tumblr and blocked bots on 2026-11-07, so only quotes reproduced elsewhere are used: estimates fail because "we tend to come up with estimates assuming that we'll be working under perfect conditions"; "The reason why people restart a project becomes obvious once you feel the temptation yourself"; the 90/10 rule credited to Tom Cargill ([LitReactor](https://litreactor.com/columns/6-game-dev-lessons-for-writers/)). 2018: "avoid falling in love with your development tools over the game you're building them for"; "it's generally better to try and finish the game *faster* and move on" ([thread](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-spelunky-i-designer-want-to-be-an-indie-then-learn-how-to-finish-games-reliably)).
- **Shippable definition:** "if it's possible for a player to beat your game without crashing it, your game is shippable"; build the ending early so the arc is complete ([Murphy, Game Developer 2015](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/finish-your-game)).
- **Rami Ismail:** attributed jam strategy (short design/prototype phase, then far longer polish) appears only second-hand on itch forums; *unverified*. No Ludum Dare keynote by any named veteran was found; *unverified*.
- **Jam postmortems:** "Cut scope early and often"; "Learn your tools beforehand" ([LD postmortem](https://b-deshidev.itch.io/in-exchange-for-my-life/devlog/162106/i-narrowly-avoided-a-disaster-this-ludum-dare-heres-what-i-learned-on-the-way)); a game whose "gameplay came from many small systems" was "pretty dangerous as a jam game" ([Blastsmith](https://zameleon.itch.io/blastsmith/devlog/94156/crafting-blastsmith-4-things-that-went-right-and-4-things-that-went-wrong)).

**Synthesis:** the minimum playable version is one mechanic, one input scheme, a title screen, 3 to 5 levels or a 5-minute loop, an end state, one sound per action, one music track. Target it for day 10. Everything after is a list ordered by what a 5-minute player sees first.

## 3. Iteration and playtesting

**Valve** (quotes collected with attribution in [GMTK's "Valve's Secret Weapon"](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/valves-secret-weapon)): Speyrer: "Playtesting is where we make the vast majority of our most important changes"; Portal was tested one week into development. Ambinder: "We see our game designs as hypotheses and our playtests as experiments." Birdwell: "Nothing is quite so humbling as being forced to watch in silence as some poor playtester stumbles around your level." Observers give no hints. Portal's GLaDOS came from testers calling the early game "a tutorial" (Walker). Faliszek (2008): "We got idiots for playtesters" is the first stage; then "It's not their fault, it's our fault"; "Playtesting is just proof to you" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/valve-s-faliszek-playtesters-aren-t-idiots-it-s-you)). Portal commentary (Swift): testers grasped portals faster when they "caught a glimpse of themselves through a portal" ([commentary transcript, fan wiki](https://combineoverwiki.net/wiki/Developer_commentary/Portal)).

**Yu, "Getting Feedback":** players know how they feel, not why; "If a large number of players are complaining about the same thing", change that area but not necessarily as suggested, since suggested fixes "are often blunt"; friends are sympathetic, strangers "better represent the general public"; convention players are "primed to have a good time" ([feedback](https://www.derekyu.com/makegames/feedback.html)).

**Celeste:** balance came from "Making our friends come over and play very rough segments of the game, over and over. And over." (Berry, [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2018/01/feature_conquering_the_indie_mountain_with_celeste_creator_matt_makes_games)).

**Failure:** "never played the final iteration of my game until AFTER I submitted it"; balancing "relied on guessing"; feedback "the game started too slow" ([LD44](https://itch.io/devlog/79103/ludum-dare-44-postmortem-the-devil-and-some-aliens-went-down-to-georgia.amp)).

**Synthesis:** test with a new person every 2 to 3 days from day 3, on the itch web build, not the editor. Sit behind them, say nothing, note where they stall, what they try first, when they lean back. Ask afterwards only what they remember and whether they would play again. Three testers agreeing on a problem means fix it; one tester's proposed fix means ignore the fix, keep the problem.

## 4. Level design for a short game

- **Hayashida (Super Mario 3D Land/World):** levels follow four-panel kishōtenketsu: "introduce a concept, and then in the next panel you develop the idea a little bit more", "in the third panel there's something of a change-up, and then in the fourth panel you have your conclusion"; "First, you have to learn how to use that gameplay mechanic, and then the stage will offer you a slightly more complicated scenario" ([Game Developer, Nutt, 2012](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-secret-to-i-mario-i-level-design)).
- **Thorson, "Level Design Workshop: Designing Celeste", GDC 2017:** session covers tools and process for 300+ stages, arranging area maps, each world feeling mechanically distinct, story in the world ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024307/Level-Design-Workshop-Designing-Celeste); free on GDC YouTube, *video content not checked*). Thorson's 2020 list of forgiveness mechanics (coyote time, jump and dash corner correction) is quoted in secondary coverage only ([Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20200317-celeste-action-feel-things)); the original thread was not fetched.
- **Portal commentary:** room 00 trains button and box first; two chambers were inserted before chamber 06 to make energy-ball training "more gradual"; the game is "an extended player training exercise" (Lovejoy) ([transcript](https://combineoverwiki.net/wiki/Developer_commentary/Portal)).
- **Jam failure:** "we should have dedicated a little more time planning out how to guide the player better"; most players skip itch-page instructions ([Blastsmith](https://zameleon.itch.io/blastsmith/devlog/94156/crafting-blastsmith-4-things-that-went-right-and-4-things-that-went-wrong)).

**Synthesis:** no text tutorial. Level 1 cannot be failed and requires the mechanic once. Each later level introduces one new thing in a safe spot, then combines it, then twists it. Every death should be the player's and recoverable in under 3 seconds (instant restart, checkpoint per room).

## 5. Finishing and polish

- **Yu, "Death Loops":** Loop of Restarting (skills improve "enough that they couldn't bear to finish the game"): scope by past finishes, polish last, shelve and start smaller if the urge persists. Loop of Polishing: "polishing a game endlessly" late in development; polish rarely widens appeal, core concept and execution do ([deathloops](https://www.derekyu.com/makegames/deathloops.html)).
- **"Juice it or lose it", Jonasson and Purho, GDC Europe 2012 (Independent Games Summit):** live demo of "neat little tricks you can apply to any game to make it more satisfying" on a Breakout clone, source code included ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1016487/Juice-It-or-Lose)). The specific technique list (tweening, screenshake, particles, sound, colour) is from the video and secondary recreations; *not verified against a transcript*.
- **"The Art of Screenshake", Nijman, Control Conference Amsterdam, Nov 2013:** ~30 tweaks to a dull shooter, e.g. "the screen shake slightly in the opposite direction of the player's gunfire", "a 33% chance of enemies exploding harmlessly on death" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games); video youtube.com/watch?v=AJdEqssNZ-U, *not checked*).
- **Godot's own tutorial** ends with background colour, a death sound, looping music, and a keyboard shortcut to start ([Finishing up](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/getting_started/first_2d_game/07.finishing-up.html)).
- **Jam postmortems:** "polish is what will separate your game from the rest" ([LD](https://b-deshidev.itch.io/in-exchange-for-my-life/devlog/162106/i-narrowly-avoided-a-disaster-this-ludum-dare-heres-what-i-learned-on-the-way)); "small animations, particles, screen effects, and shakes" listed under what went right ([Blastsmith](https://zameleon.itch.io/blastsmith/devlog/94156/crafting-blastsmith-4-things-that-went-right-and-4-things-that-went-wrong)).

**Synthesis (feature freeze):** freeze features at day 14 (Nov 24). Days 15 to 19: juice, audio, menus, itch page, README. Days 20 to 21: bug fixes only, re-test the uploaded build on two browsers, submit a day early.

## 6. Godot 4 specifics that reduce risk

Source for all of this section unless noted: [Exporting for the Web](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html).

- **Version:** 4.7.2-stable, 18 Aug 2026; 4.8 is in beta ([archive](https://godotengine.org/download/archive/)). The 4.7 page says to read the migration guide before upgrading ([4.7](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.7/)).
- **Renderer:** web needs WebAssembly and WebGL 2.0; only Compatibility works; Safari "has several issues with WebGL 2.0". **Language:** "Projects written in C# using Godot 4 currently cannot be exported to the web." Use GDScript.
- **Threads:** untick Thread Support. Single-threaded export is "the preferred and now default way", "more compatible overall with stores like" itch.io and Poki. Threaded builds need `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin` and `Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy: require-corp` or "the project will not run". itch.io offers this as Embed Options > Frame Options > "SharedArrayBuffer support", still labelled experimental, and it breaks iframes and some Safari/Firefox flows ([itch thread, 2022 to 2026](https://itch.io/t/2025776/experimental-sharedarraybuffer-support)). Avoid it.
- **Audio:** since 4.3 web uses Web Audio "sample" playback by default: low latency without threads, but no AudioEffects, reverb, doppler or procedural audio, and positional audio is imperfect. Stream mode restores features with more latency. Browsers block autoplay, so the player must click first (itch's default "Click to Play" covers this). Precompress or keep assets small: itch does not compress on the fly; `.wasm` gzips to about a quarter.
- **Other traps:** `user://` saves need IndexedDB/cookies allowed, fail in private windows; background tabs pause; fullscreen must come from an input callback; gamepads appear only after a button press; editing the exported HTML is lost on re-export, use a custom shell.
- **itch.io HTML upload:** ZIP with `index.html` at root, relative paths, case-sensitive names, max 1,000 files and 500 MB; Embed in page with fixed viewport or click-to-fullscreen; optional fullscreen button; test after upload, a 403 means a folder or case mismatch ([itch docs](https://itch.io/docs/creators/html5)).
- **Project layout:** group assets near the scenes that use them; snake_case files, PascalCase nodes; third-party code in `addons/`; `.gdignore` to exclude folders ([Project organization](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/best_practices/project_organization.html)). **Version control:** tick Git in the New Project dialog to generate `.gitignore` and `.gitattributes` (LF endings); ignore `.godot/`; set up Git LFS before the first commit if you track binaries ([Version control systems](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/best_practices/version_control_systems.html)).
- **Learning path:** "Your first 2D game" is seven pages: project setup, player scene, player code, enemy, main scene, HUD, finishing up ([index](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/getting_started/first_2d_game/index.html)). Do it once before the jam.
- **CI:** `godot --headless --export-release "Web" build/web/index.html`; the preset must exist in `export_presets.cfg`, templates must be installed ([command line](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/editor/command_line_tutorial.html)). godot-ci's reference workflow uses container `barichello/godot-ci:<version>`, moves templates into `~/.local/share/godot/export_templates/`, exports Web, uploads an artifact and deploys `build/web` to `gh-pages` with JamesIves/github-pages-deploy-action ([workflow](https://github.com/abarichello/godot-ci/blob/master/.github/workflows/godot-ci.yml)). The Dockerfile default is 4.5; use a tag matching your editor ([Dockerfile](https://github.com/abarichello/godot-ci/blob/master/Dockerfile); 4.7 tag availability *unverified*). Note GitHub Pages lacks COOP/COEP headers, another reason to stay single-threaded. itch deployment via butler is documented in godot-ci for GitLab only; port it or upload by hand.

## Suggested 3-week shape (synthesis, not sourced)

- **Days 1 to 3:** repo, godot-ci, empty web build live on itch; mechanic prototype; two testers.
- **Days 4 to 10:** minimum playable version, start to end, placeholder art, instant restart; tester every 2 to 3 days.
- **Days 11 to 14:** levels in introduce/develop/twist/conclude order; feature freeze Nov 24.
- **Days 15 to 19:** juice, audio, menus, itch page, README with GIF.
- **Days 20 to 21:** bugs only; test uploaded build in Chrome and Firefox; submit Nov 30.

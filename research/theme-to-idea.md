# From jam theme to a pitchable idea: how practitioners ideate and choose

Researched 2026-11-07. Scope: solo developer, new to game dev, Godot 4, browser build, 3-week jam (Game Off 2026, theme SHIFT, due Dec 1 2026), rated on Gameplay, Graphics, Audio, Innovation and Theme interpretation. Only ideation is covered: theme to a chosen idea. Jam rules, rating mechanics and the idea-to-shipped process are in `game-off-2026-how-to-win.md` and `idea-to-shipped-game.md` and are not repeated. Claims cite primary sources (the practitioners' own talks, slides, posts, jam pages, results tables); anything marked *unverified* could not be traced to a primary record. Section 6 is the agent's own synthesis and is labelled as such.

## The 10 rules

1. **Dump the obvious interpretations first, then throw them away.** Chevy Ray Johnston's method, as relayed by Christer Kaitila and Stas Korotaev: list the first few concepts, then discard them, because many other entrants will build exactly those ([Korotaev](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/coming-up-with-ideas-for-a-game-jam-game), [Kaitila excerpt](https://www.packtpub.com/en-us/learning/how-to-tutorials/dealing-game-jam-theme/)). Mark Brown's jam recaps show why: "so many games about resizing windows" for Built to Scale, "a sea of games about repeating the same day" for Loop, "many games that used the literal digits of a countdown clock" for Count Down ([2024](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-best-games-from-gmtk-game-jam-500), [2025](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/my-favourite-games-from-gmtk-game), [2026](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/my-favourite-games-from-gmtk-game-50b)).
2. **Look for a cheeky alternative definition of the word.** Brown: "there's usually a way to be cheeky and find some alternative definition for one of the words", and "it's totally fine for people to interpret the words of the theme in whatever way they like" ([2026](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/my-favourite-games-from-gmtk-game-50b), [2024](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-best-games-from-gmtk-game-jam-500)). Game Off's own announcement lists gear shift, perspective shift, the Shift key, night shift, blame shift and bit shift as equally valid ([GitHub blog](https://github.blog/open-source/gaming/game-off-2026-the-theme-is-shift/)).
3. **Put the theme in the verb, not the backdrop.** Game Off 2025's top Theme interpretation entries all make waves the mechanic: La Ola (typing drives a stadium wave), Sineonara (you *are* a sine wave), Castle Splash (waves knock down sandcastles) ([results](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2025/results/theme-interpretation), [La Ola](https://conorwilson.itch.io/la-ola), [Sineonara](https://itch.io/post/14964162/view-in-topic), [Castle Splash](https://macmist.itch.io/game-off-2025)). Koster: do not ask "what is the game about?"; ask "what is the mechanic about, and how does the player do that?" ([Practical Creativity slides](https://www.raphkoster.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Practical-Creativity.pdf)).
4. **Start from one mechanic and ask what it forces.** Blow's Braid began as the question of how a game changes with unlimited rewind and no fear of death, with every puzzle tied to that ability; he then chose a "simple and familiar" genre so the untested idea would not lose players ([Taipei 2019 talk summary](https://gamedesignaspect.blogspot.com/2019/07)). Game Off's page gives the same tip: "Start with one mechanic and one playable level" ([itch jam page](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2026)).
5. **Build ideas by collision: mash two systems, swap an input, add a verb, change a dimension.** Koster's toolbox: "A meets B" (Lemmings meets Canabalt), "Change input mappings" (a joystick into a button gives Soccer Physics), "Add a verb or goal" (shooter plus rescuing gives Defender), "Change a dimension", "Force constraints", "Transplant skins" ([slides](https://www.raphkoster.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Practical-Creativity.pdf)). Yu's Spelunky came from combining platforming, roguelike level generation and destructible terrain, after which "the rest of the design was easy to settle on" ([Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-derek-yu-s-i-spelunky-i-)).
6. **Pick the clear idea over the cool one, and check three things.** A GMTK 2025 team ran every candidate through technical feasibility, art scope and "Is it fun for the player to play?", and advises: "go with the one that is clear over the one that feels 'cooler'" ([Pinch & Repeat postmortem](https://fireflies-studios.itch.io/pinch-and-repeat/devlog/1005513/gmtk-game-jam-2025-post-mortem)). Fullerton's Game Design Workshop process narrows the brainstorm to "the top three" before prototyping (course slides based on the book; book text *unverified*: [slides](https://www.slideshare.net/dmullich/lafs-game-design-6-conceptualization)).
7. **You cannot judge fun on paper. Prototype the finalist within a day.** "You have to prove the fun of any idea, you can't discount it until you've played it" ([Pinch & Repeat](https://fireflies-studios.itch.io/pinch-and-repeat/devlog/1005513/gmtk-game-jam-2025-post-mortem)). Doug Wilson knew J.S. Joust was right when the room was laughing: "At the point where we were really laughing, we knew we were in a good spot" ([Kill Screen](https://www.killscreen.com/process-not-product-conversation-doug-wilson/)).
8. **Time-box ideation to a small fraction of the jam.** Rami Ismail's "4:44" rule (Nordic Game Jam 2016, relayed secondhand): design and prototyping first, then roughly ten times as long on polish ([itch relay](https://itch.io/post/4367831/view-in-topic)); primary recording *unverified*. For three weeks that is about two days of ideation and prototyping. Postmortems agree: two hours of brainstorming, sleep, decide in the morning ([Pinch & Repeat](https://fireflies-studios.itch.io/pinch-and-repeat/devlog/1005513/gmtk-game-jam-2025-post-mortem)); "focus on mechanics rather than story on the first day" and "don't be afraid to reject ideas" ([Breaking the Cycle](https://shadowlt.itch.io/breaking-the-cycle/devlog/1007367/gmtk-game-jam-2025-devlog-i-made-a-game)).
9. **Scrap an idea the moment play shows it is not fun, and keep the systems.** Taro Omiya: be ready to scrap "the moment you notice the game won't be fun"; he did so twice ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/some-advice-on-global-game-jam-2013)). A jammer who dropped a platformer after about ten hours reused its movement and collision code in a new design and shipped bigger than expected ([itch post](https://itch.io/post/13048489/view-in-topic)). Wilson's Joust started as a racing game and became a fighting game because pushing each other was more fun ([Kill Screen](https://www.killscreen.com/process-not-product-conversation-doug-wilson/)).
10. **Add a twist to a familiar base rather than inventing a genre.** Brown praises jam games that put "a tactical spin on Katamari" or add "an excellent new wrinkle to the Spacechem formula" and criticises one for lacking "an extra twist or idea" ([2024](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-best-games-from-gmtk-game-jam-500), [2026](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/my-favourite-games-from-gmtk-game-50b)). Koster: a new stat or rule already makes a variant; most creative work is "within genres, with simple variants at best" ([slides](https://www.raphkoster.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Practical-Creativity.pdf)).

## 1. Documented ideation methods

**Raph Koster, "Practical Creativity" (GDC Next 2014).** The most concrete primary catalogue found. Creativity is "serendipitous collisions of elements typically not associated with one another" and "almost never 'the creation of something truly new'". Methods, in his slide titles: *Atomization and abstraction* (treat each input and subsystem as its own game; "build your mechanics library"); *Context replacement* ("a die is an interface": token, countdown, state); *Add a statistic* ("by far the simplest" route to a variant; removing a stat or verb also works); *Change a dimension*; *Force constraints* ("Think of Tetris without the tetrominoes"); *Add a verb or goal* ("The most interesting gameplay comes when the player's goals are somehow contradictory"); *Change input mappings*; *Change topologies*; *A meets B*; *Simulate using an oddity* ("Can you do jumping... with dice?"); *Transplant skins* (Pac-Man's "visit every node" becomes painting in Bristles); *Merge a mechanic*. On theme he gives two directions: *Top down, experience first* (name the feeling, break it into verbs, find mechanics with analogous actions, risk of "generic mechanics") and *Bottom up, system first* (find a metaphor for the system, then "move back and forth between the system and the theme"). Closing slide: "Be a stranger to the familiar. Learn to atomize and abstract. Build a diverse pattern library. Move patterns between contexts. Give yourself constraints." He also says classic no-criticism brainstorming "is bad because it lacks risk" and allowing criticism "generates more ideas" ([slides PDF](https://www.raphkoster.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Practical-Creativity.pdf), [talk page](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/presentations/practical-creativity/)).

**Jonathan Blow, mechanic first.** Braid: unlimited rewind as the premise, all puzzles derived from it, familiar platformer wrapper chosen deliberately ([2019 talk summary](https://gamedesignaspect.blogspot.com/2019/07)). GMTK's video on his puzzle design describes the same for The Witness (line-drawing rule explored for consequences); transcript not fetchable, *unverified* wording.

**Derek Yu, genre combination.** Spelunky grew from post-Aquaria prototypes "for simple platformers and roguelikes"; the level generator came from a roguelike prototype; combining platforming, random generation and destructible terrain settled the rest ([Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-derek-yu-s-i-spelunky-i-)).

**Daniel Cook (Lostgarden).** "What's the simplest rule set that results in the most gameplay?" He prototypes to find the game first and then adds world, theme and characters that fit the mechanics; team ideation is "creative tennis"; he abandoned a prototype that "was not very Danc-like"; he says he "only rarely design[s] like a truffle pig, rooting for fun" ([What is a Daniel Cook game?](https://lostgarden.com/2023/07/08/what-is-a-daniel-cook-game/)).

**Zach Gage, friction first.** He starts from "the thing that kept me from getting invested in a game", looks for "extremely low-hanging fruit" in disrespected genres, and picks the vehicle for practicality: chess "happened to be the easiest thing to take apart" for a statement about fairness ([Juul interview](https://jesperjuul.net/handmadepixels/interviews/gage.html)).

**Jesse Schell, The Art of Game Design.** The 3rd edition's table of lenses lists Lens #14 "The Lens of the Problem Statement" followed by #15 "The Eight Filters" (O'Reilly table of lenses, seen in search snippet; page itself returned 403). A "Lens of Infinite Inspiration" could not be confirmed by name: *unverified*. The book's inspiration advice is summarised secondhand as "look everywhere else besides other games" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/inspiration-quot-look-everywhere-else-quot-jesse-schell)). Brainstorming tip text not fetched.

**Tracy Fullerton, Game Design Workshop.** Chapter 6 (Conceptualization; "Ideation" in the 5th edition). Course slides built on the book list "State a Challenge, No Criticism, Vary The Method, Playful Environment, Put It On The Wall, Go For Lots of Ideas, Don't Take Too Long" and a brainstorming step that ends "Narrow down the list to the top three" ([slides](https://www.slideshare.net/dmullich/lafs-game-design-6-conceptualization)). Book wording *unverified*.

**Christer Kaitila, The Game Jam Survival Guide (Packt 2012).** Covers theme handling and brainstorming in its first 12-hour phase and quotes Chevy Ray Johnston's "make a really simple game, and spend all your time polishing it"; the publisher excerpt notes winners often use the theme word "to find endless material for humor" (publisher page returned 403 on fetch; claims rest on search snippets of the Packt excerpt: *partly unverified*, [book](https://subscription.packtpub.com/book/game-development/9781849692502/pref06)).

**Stas Korotaev (Game Developer blog, 2015).** Use the theme as a constraint and add your own if it is vague ("a shoot-'em-up where the player can't shoot"); warm up on past Ludum Dare themes; mock up the interface and "mentally play the game" before building; rank genres by MVP difficulty; "3d games normally take up approximately 4x more time than 2d ones" ([article](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/coming-up-with-ideas-for-a-game-jam-game)). Blogger, credentials not stated.

Not found as primary sources: Tynan Sylvester on where ideas come from, Chris Crawford, Bennett Foddy on jam selection, Vlambeer on how Wasteland Kings was chosen (press kit says only that it was made during MOJAM 2013, [itch](https://vlambeer.itch.io/)), the Celeste jam "why" (itch page says "made in four days", [Celeste Classic](https://maddymakesgamesinc.itch.io/celesteclassic)). All *unverified*.

## 2. Theme interpretation: literal vs lateral, mechanic vs fiction

**Organiser stance.** Game Off: "How you interpret the theme is entirely up to you", "Strong or loose inspirations apply!", "Don't overthink it. Pick an interpretation that interests you and see where it takes you" ([GitHub blog](https://github.blog/open-source/gaming/game-off-2026-the-theme-is-shift/)). Ludum Dare: the theme exists to fix the "blank canvas" problem; "Using the theme is a recommendation, not a requirement" ([why](https://ludumdare.com/resources/questions/why-have-a-theme), [required?](https://ludumdare.com/resources/questions/is-the-theme-required)). GMTK has no Theme category; its criteria are Creativity, Enjoyment, Narrative, Artwork, Audio ([GMTK 2025](https://itch.io/jam/gmtk-2025)).

**Game Off winners, how the theme shows up (organiser descriptions).**

| Year, theme | Overall #1 | Theme in #1 | Theme interpretation #1 | Theme in that entry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 WAVES | Evaw | Mechanic and fiction: "light waves, sound waves, and poetic radio transmissions guide you" | La Ola (also #9 overall) | Mechanic: typing keeps a stadium wave going ([La Ola](https://conorwilson.itch.io/la-ola)) |
| 2024 SECRETS | Trail of Secrets | Mechanic/structure: "hidden paths and secret endings" | Trail of Secrets | same |
| 2023 SCALE | Glory to SCALE | Fiction: "the totalitarian state SCALE" | a Waltz in SPACE | Mechanic: "scaling planets' gravitational forces" |

Sources: [2025 post](https://github.blog/open-source/gaming/light-waves-rising-tides-and-drifting-ships-game-off-2025-winners/), [2024 post](https://github.blog/open-source/game-off-2024-winners/), [2023 post](https://github.blog/open-source/gaming/game-off-2023-results/), results tables [2025](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2025/results/theme-interpretation), [2024](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2024/results/theme-interpretation), [2023](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2023/results/theme-interpretation).

Of the 2025 top ten overall, eight are described by the organiser with waves as a mechanic (water level as level design, sound waves as weapons, waves reshaping a board, ripple physics) and one as pure fiction (The Last Wave: "waves carry memory, grief, and identity"). The 2025 Theme interpretation top ten is dominated by mechanic-level readings, including lateral ones: a sine wave as the player (Sineonara: "I interpreted 'Wave' as both a Sine wave and Waves of enemies"), a Mexican wave, radio wavelengths (WAVE / LENGTH, "A game about the waves we make", [page](https://pooglies.itch.io/wave-length)). 2023's top ten by theme is full of puns on the word (Banana for Scale, ScaleGun, Scales of Fate), and the overall winner's story-level use ranked only 9th on theme.

**Does mechanic-level use score higher in Theme interpretation?** No organiser statement or published analysis exists: *unverified*. The observation above (three years, top-ten lists) is consistent with it but is the agent's reading, not data. Castle Splash shows the categories are independent: 3rd on theme, 129th overall ([results](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2025/results/theme-interpretation)).

**Mark Brown on theme use.** He asked for literal readings of Built to Scale ("teeny tiny things - like mould and ants") and still praised lateral ones: fish scales (Scale The Depths) and climbing (Blueprint Bob, "a smart use of the theme") ([2024](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-best-games-from-gmtk-game-jam-500)). For Count Down he praised a "Count" Dracula pun as cheeky and repeatedly scolded games that smuggled in counting up: "We're counting up again! Cut it out!" ([2026](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/my-favourite-games-from-gmtk-game-50b)). For Loop he singled out PhotoLoop, built on "the looping lasso tool in Photoshop", and a power strip plugged into itself: "Talk about thinking outside of the box" ([2025](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/my-favourite-games-from-gmtk-game)). Pattern: lateral readings win attention when the theme still drives the rule set; the twist must not quietly abandon the theme.

## 3. Selection criteria used by practitioners

- **Three checks, then clarity.** Technical feasibility, art scope, player engagement; "go with the one that is clear over the one that feels 'cooler'"; chosen "because of the simple scope in terms of coding and animations" ([Pinch & Repeat](https://fireflies-studios.itch.io/pinch-and-repeat/devlog/1005513/gmtk-game-jam-2025-post-mortem)).
- **Simplest rules, most gameplay.** Cook's question, plus content leverage through "reuse, variation, and procedural generation" ([Lostgarden](https://lostgarden.com/2023/07/08/what-is-a-daniel-cook-game/)). Koster's fractal check: "What system is this merely a component in?" ([slides](https://www.raphkoster.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Practical-Creativity.pdf)).
- **Untested mechanic, familiar genre.** Blow's Braid choice ([summary](https://gamedesignaspect.blogspot.com/2019/07)). Yu's risk advice (familiar genre, little one-off content) is in `idea-to-shipped-game.md`.
- **Laughter in the room.** Wilson ([Kill Screen](https://www.killscreen.com/process-not-product-conversation-doug-wilson/)). Brown: "make them smile, make them laugh" ([2024](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-best-games-from-gmtk-game-jam-500)).
- **Simple, one mechanic to build on.** Omiya: "simple, Angry Birds-style", and drop any feature that takes "more than an hour" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/some-advice-on-global-game-jam-2013)). Korotaev: no 3D, no multiplayer, mentally play the loop for "enough meaningful choices" and downtime ([article](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/coming-up-with-ideas-for-a-game-jam-game)).
- **Practical vehicle for the concept.** Gage: the easiest thing to take apart ([Juul](https://jesperjuul.net/handmadepixels/interviews/gage.html)).
- **Not found:** Hecker/Gingold on choosing what to prototype (their prototyping heuristics are in the other file), Adam Saltsman on why Canabalt's idea (sources conflict on the EGP theme wording, "Minimalism" vs "Bare Minimum": *unverified*), Celeste and Vlambeer idea-selection write-ups. Folklore such as "fun in 10 seconds" and "explain it in one sentence without 'and'" appears only in community guides, not in a named practitioner's primary text: *folklore*.

## 4. What voters reward in Innovation and Theme

- **Game Off defines neither category**; itch lists them as names only ([jam page](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2026)). Game Off 2025's Innovation #1 was Waveform Generator (25 ratings) and #2 Approve Please; Evaw tied 3rd ([results](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2025/results/innovation)). Innovation and Theme top tens overlap heavily (Waveform Generator, Sineonara, wey, Evaw, Froggy Love in both), which suggests voters read a theme-driven mechanic as innovative. Correlation not published: *unverified*.
- **Mark Brown's curation criteria** (his picks weight Creativity and Enjoyment, [GMTK 2025](https://itch.io/jam/gmtk-2025)): a twist on a known formula, polish, "simple but smart", humour, "not all style, no substance", and he notes "presentation doesn't always matter in game jams" citing a game with no audio ([2025](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/my-favourite-games-from-gmtk-game), [2026](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/my-favourite-games-from-gmtk-game-50b)).
- **Ludum Dare** states theme use is a "social expectation", not a rule ([FAQ](https://ludumdare.com/resources/questions/is-the-theme-required)). Any claim that lateral themes score higher is *folklore*.

## 5. Time-boxing ideation and pivoting

- **Ismail's 4:44** (secondhand, [itch relay](https://itch.io/post/4367831/view-in-topic)): a 72-hour variant is "7 hours of design and prototyping followed by 65 hours of polish". Scaled to 21 days: about 2 days.
- **Two hours, sleep, decide** ([Pinch & Repeat](https://fireflies-studios.itch.io/pinch-and-repeat/devlog/1005513/gmtk-game-jam-2025-post-mortem)). **Mechanics before story on day one**; an hour spent drafting a story intro "wouldn't go anywhere" ([Breaking the Cycle](https://shadowlt.itch.io/breaking-the-cycle/devlog/1007367/gmtk-game-jam-2025-devlog-i-made-a-game)).
- **Pivot rules:** scrap when play shows no fun ([Omiya](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/some-advice-on-global-game-jam-2013)); keep engine-level systems separate from the design so a pivot reuses them ([itch post](https://itch.io/post/13048489/view-in-topic)); follow the fun the room finds, even into another genre ([Wilson](https://www.killscreen.com/process-not-product-conversation-doug-wilson/)). Koster: "Assume that most creative attempts will FAIL. This is the norm." ([slides](https://www.raphkoster.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Practical-Creativity.pdf)).
- **Game Off's own tip:** "Start with one mechanic and one playable level" ([jam page](https://itch.io/jam/game-off-2026)).

## 6. Worked example on SHIFT (agent's synthesis, not sourced)

**Interpretations, grouped by sense.**
Physical: 1 shift blocks on a grid (sokoban), 2 shifting sands that move the level each turn, 3 tectonic plates that split the map, 4 weight shift to balance a raft. Temporal: 5 time shift between two eras of one room, 6 a night shift that lasts until the clock hits morning, 7 shift work rota as a scheduling puzzle. Perspective: 8 camera rotation turns walls into floors, 9 shift between first and third person, 10 shift between two overlapping realities. Shapeshifting: 11 take enemy forms with their weaknesses, 12 shift between solid, liquid, gas. Mechanical: 13 gear shift where each gear changes a rule (gravity, time, brakes), 14 phase shift between in-phase waves to pass barriers, 15 colour shift (hue cycling reveals platforms). Linguistic and input: 16 the Shift key as the only control, 17 Caps-Lock typing game where Shift flips word meaning, 18 cipher game where letters shift (Caesar), 19 bit shifts resize objects. Social: 20 shift the blame in an interrogation, 21 shifting alliances in a tiny diplomacy game, 22 cover-my-shift negotiation adventure, 23 paradigm shift: rules of the game change when enough players believe. Systems: 24 supply chain where demand shifts each round, 25 shift register puzzle moving tokens along a chain, 26 red shift / blue shift as a scanning mechanic, 27 shift the goalposts: the win condition moves when you approach it.

**Method A, discard the obvious (Chevy Ray via Kaitila and Korotaev).** The announcement itself lists gear shift, perspective puzzler, two realities, shapeshifter, night-shift horror, blame shift, bit shift and Shift-key-only, so expect crowds there (rule 1). Strike 8, 10, 11, 13, 16, 19, 20, 22. Remaining candidates with a lateral but recognisable reading: 2, 6, 7, 14, 17, 18, 24, 25, 26, 27.

**Method B, Koster's "what is the mechanic about, and how does the player do that?" plus the three checks.** For each survivor, name the verb and the rule it forces. 2 shifting sands: verb "step", rule "every move slides the terrain one cell", clear, feasible in Godot TileMap, fun uncertain but testable in a day. 14 phase shift: verb "tune", rule "match frequency to pass", similar to WAVE / LENGTH last year. 17 Caps/Shift word flipping: verb "type", rule "shifted letters change the word's meaning", novel, art scope tiny, fun unproven. 25 shift register: verb "push", rule "every push moves the whole chain", clear and content-generating. 27 moving goalposts: cooler than it is clear; cut per "clear over cooler". Keep 2, 17, 25.

**Method C, Blow's "what does the mechanic force?" under Ismail's time-box.** Spend day one and two prototyping the strongest one. Shifting sands (2) forces: levels are small, every puzzle is "reach the exit while the floor flows", the theme is in the verb, and the mechanic generates its own levels from a handful of rules (Cook's content leverage). If a grey-box level is not fun to a tester by the end of day two, keep the TileMap and movement code and switch to 25, which shares them (rule 9). The pitch sentence for the finalist: "A grid puzzler where every step you take shifts the sand under the whole level."
